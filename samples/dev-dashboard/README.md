# dev-dashboard

Everything your app is doing, live, at **`/dev/dashboard`**: memory, CPU, requests per second, p50/p95/p99 latency, errors, the database pool and Tokio, as charts; every route with its numbers; every request as a trace waterfall; every SQL statement by fingerprint, with N+1 detection and EXPLAIN; the live log; an app doctor that turns all of it into ranked findings; a routes explorer that spots broken links and missing gates; the schema as an interactive diagram, with a migrations timeline that catches drift; an optional AI assistant on every tab with your own key; your test runs on SQLite and Postgres; your CI; and a load lab to make the numbers move. Phoenix has LiveDashboard and Rails has rack-mini-profiler plus a handful of gems; this is one sample, in-process, with no Prometheus, Grafana or Jaeger to run. No dependency on other samples. Extracted from Rust off Rails apps.

- **AI command center** (the top of the overview, expanded by default, collapsible and remembered in this browser): a **briefing** that needs no key (the app's state in 3 to 5 plain lines from the insights, N+1s, errors, incidents and test runs, e.g. "N+1 on /orders (20 queries per request) — fix with load_many", each with its evidence link and a sparkline) under a verdict chip (Quiet, Healthy, Watch, Unhealthy); with a key, the **AI briefing** (the agent's health summary, streamed live with citations and inline charts; written once per boot on the first visit in proactive mode, otherwise a button); a prompt box ("Ask anything about your running app…") with suggestions drawn from the findings ("Why is GET /orders slow?") and quick links; four small charts of the last 15 minutes with the briefing's findings numbered on them; and **What you can do here**, a card per capability linking to its tab. Without a key the AI parts are a setup card (Anthropic, OpenRouter's cheap models, a local model).
- **Overview**: cards with sparklines, colored by health (green, amber, red; thresholds are `HEALTH`, `// APP:`): memory (and its growth since boot), CPU, req/s, latency, server errors, SQL, pool, Tokio, uptime; the top insights, the slowest routes and queries, the latest traces and N+1s, and panels from other samples (the job queue when `jobs` is installed).
- **Charts**: ten live charts and a latency heatmap (SVG, light and dark, gradient fills), 5 min, 15 min, 1 h, 24 h, 7 days or 30 days, across restarts: the history lives on disk (below). Legends summarize the visible window (average and peak for rates, request-weighted percentiles for latency, "—" without data). A crosshair synchronized across every chart with a readout of all series; drag (or Shift+arrows) to zoom into a range; click Requests, Latency or Errors to open that minute's traces; markers for boots, load-lab runs and error bursts. All of it works from the keyboard, and every chart keeps a data table as its text alternative.
- **Requests**: per matched route: hits, 2xx/3xx/4xx/5xx, p50/p95/p99/max, bytes, last hit, the slowest examples (each a trace).
- **Traces, an explorer on one screen**: every request, **every background job and mail delivery** (the latest 300, plus the notable ones: a 5xx, an N+1, slower than 0.5 s, those also on disk). Filters as dropdowns and chips (route with hit counts, kind, status, minimum duration, N+1, errors, time range synced with the charts, text on the path, a method), all in the URL; a **duration scatter** above the list (x time, y duration, color status: drag across it to zoom, click a dot to open its trace); rows that **open inline** into their waterfall (`j`/`k` to move, Enter to open, Esc to close); **compare** two traces side by side ("#12 ran 19 more queries than #9"); new traces stream in, held while you're reading.
- **The waterfall** (the same component on the trace page, in the explorer's rows, a route's drawer on Requests, a statement's page, N+1 findings, a job's page on the jobs dashboard, the mail a request sent, and the Ask AI citations): a **collapsible tree**, one line per row (`SELECT lab_order_items WHERE order_id = ?`, the full statement highlighted in the side panel), **repeats folded** (an N+1's 20 statements, or `load_items ×20`, as one row with a mini-bar per run), the framework's own checks folded into a `framework` row, bars colored by kind (handler, method, SQL, template, job, outbound HTTP, middleware, events as markers), **self time vs total**, **uninstrumented time hatched** (where to add a span), the **critical path** marked, a header with time by kind as a stacked bar, the query and N+1 counts, the slowest step and a suggested fix. Click a row for its side panel (fields, `file:line`, the SQL with EXPLAIN, Ask about this, copy as JSON). Expand all, collapse all, "collapse repeated SQL"; the state stays in the URL hash. Without JavaScript every row is there (`?all=1`).
- **Methods**: every span aggregated, profiler style (wall clock, so waits on the database count): calls, total and self time, p50/p95/max, share of request time, the routes that call it, callers and callees; click one for the traces that ran it. An **icicle** (flame graph, top down) of a route's traces merged. **Instrumenting your own methods** is one attribute (below).
- **Hot functions** (development, Linux and macOS): a low-rate **CPU sampling profiler** (99 Hz, `pprof`) you start from the page (it samples until you stop it, 30 minutes at most; `DASHBOARD_PROFILER=always` from boot), with no annotations at all: functions by self and total share, the usual path to each, callers and callees, **your code vs dependencies vs std/Tokio vs native**, the routes that were being served, and a flame graph. Pause and resume from the page; never in production.
- **History on disk**: the charts' minutes (and hourly rollups), their markers, the notable traces and every job or mail run go to `storage/dashboard/history.sqlite3` (its own SQLite file, compressed, `0600`), so a restart loses nothing but the minute in progress, and memory keeps only the hot window. 7 days of minutes, 90 days of hours, 14 days of notable traces, 64 MB at most (`// APP:`). About 70 KB of disk a day of history.
- **SQL**: statements grouped by fingerprint (literals stripped): count, total, mean, p95, max, last seen, the routes that ran it, parameters as type and length only. The **N+1** list (the same statement more than 5 times in one request) with route and trace. **Writes during a GET.** **EXPLAIN** (SQLite `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN`, Postgres `EXPLAIN`) for recorded SELECTs.
- **Logs**: a live tail over Server-Sent Events, requests and log lines together (like Phoenix's request logger), filtered by level, target and text, with pause and resume. Secret-looking fields are `[redacted]`.
- **Insights**: findings ranked by impact, each with its evidence, a fix, the framework guide that explains it and the page that proves it (an N+1's waterfall opens right there): N+1 hotspots, slow routes and queries, **slow functions** (a method past its p95 threshold, one run in a loop, one getting slower, an app function hot on the CPU), rising errors, foreign keys without an index, full scans from EXPLAIN, large or fast-growing tables, pending migrations, jobs that keep failing, memory that keeps growing, a saturated pool, slow renders, and **CSP violations** the browser reports. Dismiss one for the session. `APP:` hook for your own rules.
- **Routes**: the route table grouped by area and resource, with each handler's source file and line, its access level and middleware, live hits, error rate, p50/p95 and a sparkline, links to its traces and SQL, a Try it link, and the problems: 5xx or rising errors, slow, never hit, no test mentions it, a mutating route without CSRF, an admin or dev path without a gate, a GET that writes, **404s seen** with the route they probably meant and the page that linked there, overlapping patterns, routes missing from `docs/routes.md`.
- **Schema**: an **entity-relationship diagram** of the live database (SQLite or Postgres): table boxes with their columns, PK/FK/unique marks, foreign keys as edges with crow's-foot cardinality (a nullable foreign key is zero-or-one, a unique one is one-to-one). Laid out on the server (layered by foreign-key depth, crossings reduced with a barycenter pass), so it reads well without JavaScript; with it, pan, zoom, drag tables (remembered in this browser, with Reset layout), click or focus a table to light up its neighbours and see its detail, find a table, "keys only", colour by sample or by name prefix. Export as SVG or as Mermaid `erDiagram` text (copy button), for docs or an LLM. Below it, the text alternative: every table with its row count (and size on Postgres), columns, indexes and foreign keys, and the relationships in words. Each table has a page: neighbours, the recorded queries that touch it, the migration that created it. On a phone the diagram scrolls in its frame and the lists carry the rest. Never a row's data.
- **Migrations** (a tab of Schema): every migration newest first, applied or pending, when, which sample or area, its file, and what it did (tables, columns and indexes created, altered or dropped, **derived** from the migration's source and labelled as such); pending ones get the host's Run button. **Drift**: tables or columns the applied migrations don't explain, ones they create that are missing, migrations applied but unknown to the code, and a migration file **edited after it was applied** (SeaORM keeps no checksum, so the dashboard records one per applied migration the first time it sees it). Drift shows in Insights too.
- **Ask AI, an agent** (optional, your own key): on every dashboard page and, through an Ask AI bar, on the other kernel pages (the mailbox and a message, the jobs dashboard, a job and the error groups, sockets, `/dev`, the host's routes page). It **investigates with read-only tools** over what the dashboard already holds (overview and thresholds, insights, routes, before/after comparisons of any two windows, chart series, traces, SQL and EXPLAIN, logs, schema, migrations and drift, route problems, jobs and their failure groups, test results, CI, the mailbox as shapes, and the app's own source under `src/`, `migration/` and `templates/`), **streams each step live** ("Looking at the SQL… → `query_sql(sort=total)`", each result one click away), and answers with **evidence as links** (`[trace:1298]`, `[sql:…]`, `[route:GET /x]`, `[file:src/…:40]`) and the **charts it cites drawn inline**. It can **propose actions** (run a load-lab scenario, run the tests, open a page) that show as buttons you click; it never changes anything itself. Answers that propose a change come with a **fix prompt for your coding agent** (evidence, file and line, the change, the test to add, the guide). Anthropic or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (OpenAI, **OpenRouter**, Ollama, LM Studio). You see the first request before anything is sent; every investigation is kept in a **history**. Bounded: steps, tokens and time per run, a daily budget. Development only unless you turn it on behind the production view's gate.
- **Health and "what am I looking at"**: the command center's verdict and briefing (and, with a key, **Generate AI briefing**: the agent investigates and writes the verdict, what's healthy, what needs attention ranked, and what to fix first, each claim linked to its evidence), a **"since you were away" digest**, and `/dev/dashboard/explain`, a guide to every card, chart, panel and page that needs no key, with "Explain more with AI" on each.
- **Proactive** (opt-in, `DASHBOARD_AI_PROACTIVE=true`, development): a watcher checks the deterministic signals (latency spike, error burst, new N+1, memory growth, job failures, schema drift, failing tests) and posts an **incident card** to the overview and Insights; with a key the agent investigates each one once (debounced, budgeted) and the card carries its narrative, evidence and proposed actions.
- **Tests**: `app test` (or the Run tests button) runs nextest on SQLite and Postgres with JUnit output; the page shows both side by side, the duration trend, the slowest tests, failures with their output, flaky tests, line coverage from llvm-cov, and which acceptance criteria in `product/` no test cites.
- **GitHub**: the app's CI runs with their success rate by day, open pull requests and issues, the latest release, open Dependabot alerts. Cached for a minute, never fetched at boot, the token never shown.
- **Load lab**: fake data through an `APP:` seed hook, a traffic generator against the app's own address (hard caps: 200 req/s, 60 s, 32 in flight), and six scenarios that light up a panel each: slow query, N+1, panic, memory spike, slow endpoint, error burst. `app bench --rps 50 --secs 10 --route /dev/lab/orders` from a terminal.
- **Config**: settings and the app's env vars with secrets redacted, OS and build info, and what the dashboard itself holds in memory.
- **For agents**: `/dev/dashboard.json` and a `dashboard` section in `/dev/introspect.json`.
- **It costs little**: about 1 µs per request for the middleware in a release build, 6 µs for a whole development request with its SQL recorded, about 1 µs per method span (see [How it works](#how-it-works)); fixed-size memory (a test pushes 20,000 requests and 5,000 statements through it and checks every bound; traces also have a byte budget), history on disk instead of in memory; nothing at all outside development unless you turn on the production view.
- **Development only** by default (404 in production, proven by a test). Optionally a **read-only** subset at `/admin/dashboard` in production, behind the admin password or your admin accounts: no load lab, no EXPLAIN, no parameters, no logs, and traces without their log events or span fields (and Ask AI only with `DASHBOARD_AI_IN_PRODUCTION=true`).

## Adapting it

- **Instrumenting your own methods** (GUIDE: `.offrails/guides/errors-and-logging.md#logging`): one attribute on the function, and it shows in every waterfall nested with its SQL, in Methods, and in the slow-function insights:

  ```rust
  #[tracing::instrument(level = "debug", skip_all)]
  async fn load_items(db: &DatabaseConnection, order_id: i64) -> Result<Vec<order_item::Model>, DbErr> { … }
  ```

  `skip_all` keeps arguments out (a password, a user, a whole model); add a field only for what helps you debug and is neither a secret nor personal data: `fields(order_id)` then `tracing::Span::current().record("order_id", order_id)`. Fields named like secrets show as `[redacted]` anyway, and the production view never shows fields. `level = "debug"` costs nothing in production (the filter is `info` there: the span is never built) and about a microsecond in development. Name a span `handler` (with `handler = "orders::show"`) for a controller action, `render` for a template, `http` for a call out, `job` / `mail` / `cron` for the outermost span of background work (it becomes a trace of its own); everything else is a method. The waterfall shows its `file:line` from the span's metadata. Where a span has lots of hatched (uninstrumented) time, add one inside. The lab's N+1 page (`lab.rs`, `load_orders_one_by_one` → `load_items`) is the worked example.
- **History on disk**: `DASHBOARD_HISTORY=false` keeps everything in memory (as before); `DASHBOARD_HISTORY_PATH` moves the file (a mounted volume). Retention and the ceiling are `MINUTE_DAYS`, `HOUR_DAYS`, `TRACE_DAYS`, `MAX_TRACES`, `MAX_BYTES` (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/history.rs`): about 70 KB a day for the charts, a few KB per notable trace. With the production view on, production writes the file too, without span fields or log events.
- **Hot functions**: `DASHBOARD_PROFILER=false` removes the sampler, `always` starts it at boot; `FREQUENCY`, `WINDOW`, `MINUTES`, `MAX_STACKS`, `MAX_RUN` are `// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/profiler.rs`. WHY on demand: symbolizing a debug build maps its debug info, and `pprof` keeps its sample table once started (measured on the demo app: 40 MB RSS without it, 440 MB while sampling, 260 MB after stopping until a restart). "Your code" is the frames under this crate's name. On Windows the page says it isn't available (spans still work).
- **Slow functions**: `SLOW_METHOD_US`, `CALLS_PER_TRACE`, `GROWTH_MIN_US`, `HOT_CPU_SHARE` (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/insights.rs`). Repeats fold at `GROUP_MIN` (`waterfall.rs`).

- **Load-test your own pages**: point `lab::seed` (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/lab.rs`) at your models, then put your routes in the Traffic form or `app bench --route`. Drop the `lab_orders` tables with a new migration when you no longer need them.
- **A panel for your own subsystem** (realtime, a payment queue): implement `PanelProvider` and add it to `providers()` (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/panels.rs`; the realtime example is in its header).
- **Your own insight rules**: return findings from `custom_rules` (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/insights.rs`); tune the thresholds at the top of that file (slow route p95, slow query p95, table sizes).
- **The dashboard in production, read-only**: `DASHBOARD_IN_PRODUCTION=true` and `APP_ADMIN_PASSWORD` (16+ characters), or your admin accounts: with auth-core installed, the optional wiring compiles `kernel/dashboard/accounts.rs` (signed-in people are asked first; `is_admin` is the `// APP:` hook, nobody until you add a role), otherwise write your own `admin_from_session` beside `no_accounts` in `kernel/dashboard/access.rs`. Behind a proxy set `APP_TRUSTED_PROXY=true`, or every client shares one failed-password budget (a warning says so once). It records in production then (a few µs per request).
- **Send traces to a collector**: set `OTLP_EXPORT` and fill `export` (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/trace.rs`) with the opentelemetry crates.
- **Group the diagram by your own areas**: add `("billing_", "billing")` rows to `AREAS` (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/erd.rs`, first match wins); the colours are `--g0`…`--g9` (`/* APP: */` in `dashboard.css`). For a bigger schema, tune `MAX_COLUMN` and `GRID_WIDTH` there.
- **Ask AI on OpenRouter** (OpenAI-compatible, with tool calling), e.g. a cheap fast model. In `.env` (the key yours, never committed):
  ```sh
  DASHBOARD_AI_PROVIDER=openai-compatible
  DASHBOARD_AI_BASE_URL=https://openrouter.ai/api/v1
  DASHBOARD_AI_API_KEY=sk-or-…                        # your OpenRouter key
  DASHBOARD_AI_MODEL=deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash     # or openai/gpt-6-luna, z-ai/glm-5.3-flash
  DASHBOARD_AI_PRICE_IN=0.04                          # optional: USD per million tokens, for the spend estimate
  DASHBOARD_AI_PRICE_OUT=0.55
  DASHBOARD_AI_REFERER=http://localhost:3000          # optional: OpenRouter's HTTP-Referer
  DASHBOARD_AI_TITLE="My app dev dashboard"           # optional: OpenRouter's X-Title (quote values with spaces: an unquoted space stops .env loading there)
  ```
  Model ids come from OpenRouter's public list (`curl -s https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models`); pick one whose `supported_parameters` include `tools` and `tool_choice`. On 2026-10-17 all three above did, listed at $0.04 / $0.55 (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash), $0.10 / $0.50 (GPT-6 Luna) and $0.15 / $0.50 (GLM-5.3 Flash) per million input / output tokens: cents per investigation. Check each model's data policy on OpenRouter before sending your app's data to it. Token use per run is kept down on every provider: a tool result older than the last step is cut to `OLD_RESULT_CHARS` (`ai_agent.rs`; the model can call the tool again), Anthropic (and `anthropic/` models through OpenRouter) get `cache_control` marks on the system prompt and the conversation so far, and OpenAI-compatible providers such as DeepSeek cache a repeated prefix by themselves.
- **Ask AI with another provider or tighter limits**: any OpenAI-compatible endpoint works through `DASHBOARD_AI_BASE_URL` (https, or http to localhost: Ollama `http://localhost:11434/v1`, LM Studio `http://localhost:1234/v1`). Budgets and caps: the `DASHBOARD_AI_*` budget variables above; their defaults and the rest are `PER_MINUTE`, `DAILY_REQUESTS`, `DAILY_CALLS`, `DAILY_TOKENS`, `MAX_STEPS`, `RUN_TOKENS`, `RUN_TIMEOUT`, `MAX_CONTEXT_CHARS`, `MAX_ANSWER_TOKENS`, `TIMEOUT` (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/ai.rs`), and `MAX_TOOL_CHARS` and the `read_source` allowlist (`ai_tools.rs`); the prompts and guides per page are `Tab::suggestions` and `Tab::guides`, the system prompt is `system_prompt`. To send more or less about a page, edit its builder in `kernel/dashboard/ai_context.rs` (each is `// APP:`): keep to redacted data, and extend `ai_contexts_carry_no_rows_parameters_or_secrets` when you add a source.
- **Add your own tool** (say, `payments_summary`): add a `ToolDef` row to `TOOLS` in `kernel/dashboard/ai_tools.rs` (a name, the step's title, a description that says what it returns and when to use it, and a JSON schema), and an arm in `call` that returns redacted text (`ai_context::clean` for free text; shapes and counts, never values); set `core: true` if small models should get it, `read_only_view: true` only if it is safe in production. Keep it read-only: an action belongs behind a button, as a new `propose_action` kind rendered in `ai_web::action_view` and posting to an existing CSRF-checked route. Add its arguments to `every_tool_answers_within_its_cap`. Another sample's data without a compile-time dependency: read its table by name (`panels::table_exists`, `ai_context::text_rows`, as the mailbox and jobs tools do), or register an `ai_context::Source` through optional wiring (as `ai_realtime.rs` does with realtime).
- **Tune it for a small model** (a local 7–30B model, a cheap tier): `DASHBOARD_AI_TOOLS=core` offers only the core tools (overview, insights, requests, compare, traces, SQL, logs, source, proposals; the `core` flags in `TOOLS`); lower `MAX_STEPS` to 4 and `MAX_CONTEXT_CHARS` to 8,000–12,000 so the first message fits a small context window; keep the suggested prompts concrete (`Tab::suggestions`), since small models do best with a precise question; and check the model's `supported_parameters` include `tools` (a model without tool calling still answers from the page data, without steps).
- **Ask AI on more pages**: `Tab::for_page` (`ai.rs`) maps a kernel page's path to a tab (and an item); add your page there, a `Tab` variant with its suggestions and guides, and its context builder in `ai_context::build`. The bar shows on any kernel page `for_page` knows.
- **The command center**: the verdict's rules (`verdict`), the briefing's length (`MAX_BULLETS`, `MIN_BULLETS`), the suggested questions (`suggestions`), the capability cards (`guide`) and the headline are `// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/ai_home.rs`; its tint is `--cc-wash` and `--cc-edge` (`/* APP: */` in `dashboard.css`). Each tab's one-line guide is `Tab::guide_line` (`ai.rs`).
- **The watcher's signals**: tune `SPIKE_FACTOR`, `ERROR_BURST`, `JOB_FAILURES`, `DEBOUNCE`, `PER_HOUR`, and its boot warm-up (`WARMUP`: the first look a minute after boot; `BOOT_INVESTIGATIONS` in the first `BOOT_WINDOW`, the most severe first; the Ask AI panel shows the watcher's share of today's tokens) (`// APP:` in `kernel/dashboard/ai_watch.rs`), or add a signal to `detect`; the agent investigates whatever `detect` returns.
- **Ask AI in production** (read-only view, its four tabs): `DASHBOARD_AI_IN_PRODUCTION=true` with `DASHBOARD_IN_PRODUCTION=true` and an admin gate. The provider then sees production's redacted SQL shapes and route numbers: decide that with whoever owns the data, and record it in `docs/decisions.md`.
- **More history or a smaller footprint**: `RECENT`, `NOTABLE`, `TRACE_BYTES`, `LOG_LINES` (`trace.rs`), `MAX_SPANS`, `MAX_SQL` (`http.rs`), `MINUTE_POINTS` (`series.rs`), `MAX_FINGERPRINTS` (`sql.rs`), `N_PLUS_ONE` and `IGNORED` (`http.rs`), `MAX_RPS`/`MAX_SECS` (`lab.rs`); every one is `// APP:`.

## Do not change

- Dev routes exist only in development; `/admin/dashboard` only with an admin gate (`the_dashboard_is_404_in_production_by_default`, `the_production_dashboard_needs_the_admin_password`).
- Parameters show as type and length, never values; EXPLAIN only for SELECTs (`sql_parameter_values_never_reach_any_page`, `explain_runs_for_selects_only`).
- The traffic generator takes paths, never URLs (`traffic_only_targets_this_app`).
- Every ring and map has a fixed size; the unmatched-path bucket is one bucket (`the_dashboard_stays_within_its_memory_bound`).
- The GitHub token stays in its header (`the_github_token_is_never_shown_or_logged`).
- `no_store()` on both routers (`every_dashboard_response_is_no_store`).
- The CSRF probe uses a method the route doesn't have, so it never runs a handler (`a_mutating_route_without_csrf_is_spotted`).
- Styles live in `static/kernel/dashboard.css` with `light-dark()` tokens, never in `assets/app.css` (`every_dashboard_page_renders_with_its_own_assets`, `the_dashboard_stylesheet_follows_the_theme_switch`).
- Ask AI starts only from a previewed first request built from redacted data, sends it to a base URL from the environment (https or loopback, no redirects), and renders answers as escaped text (`only_the_previewed_context_is_sent`, `ai_contexts_carry_no_rows_parameters_or_secrets`, `the_ai_base_url_is_https_or_loopback`, `the_ai_client_handles_errors_timeouts_oversize_and_redirects`, `a_model_reply_with_markup_renders_inert`).
- Every tool reads; an action is only a validated proposal the human clicks; tool output is delimited as untrusted data (`a_prompt_injection_in_app_data_has_no_side_effect_and_leaks_nothing`, `proposed_actions_are_validated_and_render_as_buttons`). `read_source` keeps its allowlist (`read_source_stays_inside_the_allowlist`). Citations link only to validated dashboard pages (`citations_link_only_to_validated_dashboard_pages`). Runs stay bounded and say why they stopped (`an_agent_run_stops_at_its_budget_and_says_so`), and a run's stream belongs to its browser (`a_run_stream_is_bound_to_its_browser`).
- The overview's AI briefing starts on its own only in proactive mode, in development, once per process, inside the budget (`the_command_center_briefs_without_a_key_and_with_one`); the briefing, the graphs and the guide work without a key.
- A live run (its page and stream) is its browser's only; a finished one opens from its permalink in any browser that can open the dashboard, as the history does (`a_run_stream_is_bound_to_its_browser`).
- The AI key stays in its header (`the_ai_key_never_reaches_logs_traces_pages_or_recorded_requests`); Ask AI is 404 in production unless turned on behind the gate (`the_ai_assistant_is_404_in_production_unless_enabled_behind_the_gate`).
- `erd.rs` `route`/`glyph` and `dashboard-erd.js` `route`/`glyph` draw edges the same way: change them together (`the_layout_is_deterministic_layered_and_overlap_free`).
- The history file is `0600` in a `0700` folder, holds only what the dashboard already redacted, and in production no span fields or log events (`the_history_file_is_private_and_redacted`); a damaged one is moved aside, never fatal (`a_damaged_or_incompatible_history_file_is_moved_aside`).
- The CPU sampler never starts outside development (`the_profiler_never_runs_outside_development`).

## How it works

Everything is in this process, in one hub (`kernel/dashboard/mod.rs`, `HUB`) of fixed-size rings and maps:

| Part | Hooks in at | Records |
|------|-------------|---------|
| `http.rs` | a middleware at the router's outermost layer (outside the panic and timeout layers) | per `ROUTES` pattern: count, status classes, a log-bucketed latency histogram, bytes, the 3 slowest traces, per-minute history; 404 paths; writes during a GET |
| `sql.rs` | SeaORM's `set_metric_callback` (sea-orm 2.0.4), installed in `AppState::new` | per fingerprint: count, histogram, routes, redacted parameters; per request: every statement for the trace and the N+1 count |
| `trace.rs` | a `tracing-subscriber` layer in `main.rs` | spans and events of each request (its trace), every log line (the live tail) |
| `collector.rs` | a task `serve` starts | once a second: RSS, virtual memory, CPU (sysinfo), open files, Tokio (workers, alive tasks, global queue, busy time; more with `--cfg tokio_unstable`), the pool (size, idle, max; sqlx doesn't count waiters, so saturation is "no idle connection at max size"), requests and SQL; in memory 15 min at 1 s and 2 h at 1 min, every closed minute to the history file |
| `history.rs` | a thread of its own, started by `serve` | the history file: minutes (one row per hour: columns delta-encoded as varints with the minute's latency histograms, deflated), hourly rollups (one row per day), markers, notable traces (compressed JSON); retention and the size ceiling hourly; reads for the long ranges, zooms, permalinks and the AI tools |
| `waterfall.rs`, `methods.rs`, `traces_web.rs` | the traces | the tree (folds, self time, gaps, critical path), the explorer, compare, methods, icicles |
| `profiler.rs` | a thread `serve` starts (development, unix), sampling only when started from the page | `pprof` at 99 Hz, symbolized every 15 s into per-minute profiles (15 kept, 1,500 stacks each) |
| `insights.rs`, `route_explorer.rs`, `schema.rs` | the above, the database catalog, CSP reports | findings, route problems, the schema |
| `erd.rs`, `migrations.rs` | the catalog, `seaql_migrations`, `migration/src/*.rs` | the diagram's layout and exports; the timeline, derived effects, drift, the checksum ledger (`storage/dashboard/migration-checksums.json`) |
| `ai.rs`, `ai_context.rs`, `ai_tools.rs`, `ai_agent.rs`, `ai_watch.rs`, `ai_web.rs`, `ai_home.rs` | the above, on request (and the opt-in watcher every 10 s) | per-page contexts, prepared previews (32 for 15 minutes), the tools, live runs (16 in memory) and their history (`storage/dashboard/ai-runs/`, 50), incident cards (20), the provider client, the rate and budget |

Annotations (boot, load-lab runs, error bursts over `ERROR_BURST` per second, the demo's deploy and incident) are kept with the series (200 at most) and drawn on every chart. A request's context lives in a Tokio task-local while it runs, so the SQL callback and the tracing layer attach to it without passing anything around. The dashboard's own pages and streams run "quietly" and record nothing. **The diagram** follows the charts' approach: `erd.rs` computes the layout and draws the SVG on the server (CSS-token colours, so light and dark need nothing), and `dashboard-erd.js` (about 400 lines, no library) moves it. WHY no layout library: a layered layout with a barycenter pass is about 150 lines for a schema of tens of tables, deterministic (so tests can pin it), and needs nothing vendored or from a CDN under the strict CSP. **Ask AI** is an agent loop on the server: the panel loads its context when opened (the preview is the exact first request: system prompt, page data and every tool's schema), and Investigate posts it (`POST …/ai/runs`, CSRF-checked) to start a run in the background. Each step sends the conversation with `tools`, runs the tool calls the model asks for (Anthropic `tool_use` blocks answered by one user turn of `tool_result` blocks; OpenAI-compatible `tool_calls` answered by one `role: tool` message each; several at once is fine), and appends both in that provider's shape; the last allowed call has `tool_choice: none`, so it must answer. The page follows the run over Server-Sent Events (`GET …/ai/runs/{id}/stream`, bound to the browser that started it): each step arrives as a fragment Askama rendered (title, the call, its result one click away), then the end: the answer as escaped blocks whose citation tags became links to the dashboard's own pages, the cited charts drawn by the Charts page's code, the proposed actions as buttons, and the fix prompt. WHY a POST then a GET stream: an `EventSource` can only GET, and the POST is what CSRF protects; WHY server-rendered fragments: one escaping path for everything the model writes. Without JavaScript the same form posts to `…/ai/ask`, which waits for the run and renders the finished page. The opt-in watcher (`ai_watch.rs`) reuses the same runs for its incident cards.

Charts are SVG rendered on the server (one implementation, works without JavaScript, nothing to vendor, the strict CSP untouched; colors are CSS tokens, so the theme switch needs no redraw); `dashboard.js` swaps in re-rendered fragments from a Server-Sent Events stream (every 5 s with `prefers-reduced-motion`, never while the tab is hidden), and adds the synchronized crosshair, the readout, zoom (`?from=&to=`, within the last 24 hours) and the click into `/dev/dashboard/traces?minute=`. The 1 h range and anything longer reads the history file through `history::between` (memory wins where both have a minute, so the hot/cold boundary has no seam); legends average every sample read, not the 300 drawn.

**History on disk.** WHY its own SQLite file and not the app's database: the app's migrations stay the app's, it works the same for Postgres apps, a `db reset` doesn't wipe it, and it is one file to delete. WHY a row per hour with a compressed blob: a minute is ~30 numbers that barely change; column-wise deltas as varints, then deflate, make an hour of minutes (histograms included) 1–3 KB, and a 24 h chart 24 row reads. Writes go through a channel to the history thread (never on a request), minutes as they close, traces in batches every 5 s; a crash loses the minute in progress. The thread answers the synchronous readers (charts, insights, AI tools) in a few milliseconds. Hours are rolled up from the minutes' merged histograms, so a 30-day chart's p95 is a true p95.

**Method spans and the CPU sampler.** The tracing layer already saw every span inside a request; now each carries its `file:line`, background work gets traces of its own (a root `job`, `mail` or `cron` span outside a request; its SQL finds the trace through the subscriber's registry), and the caps are 2,000 spans, 1,000 statements and 100 events per trace, past which they are counted ("Truncated"), with an 8 MB byte budget for the rings. `method_spans_are_cheap` measures about 1 µs per span in a debug build (each is a field visit, a timestamp and a push onto its trace; a request opening 200 of them), and fails above 25 µs. The sampler (`pprof`, signal-based) costs the samples themselves (99 per second per busy thread) plus a fold every 15 s, shown on the page (tens of milliseconds, on its own thread).

**Overhead**, measured by `the_middleware_overhead_is_small` (a trivial route behind the middleware alone, recording on and off on alternate requests, compared by median over 3,600 requests): **1.2 µs per request in a release build**, about 5 µs in a debug build; the test fails above 10 µs (release) or 50 µs (debug). A whole development request through every layer, with its SQL recorded and its trace and log line written, measured 6.3 µs more (97 → 103 µs for a static page, release). With the dashboard off (production by default, and tests) the middleware checks one atomic flag.
